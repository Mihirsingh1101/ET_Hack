CHART PATTERNS KNOWLEDGE BASE
================================

This document covers the major chart patterns used in technical analysis of stocks and indices.

---

PATTERN: RESISTANCE BREAKOUT
Type: Breakout
Direction: Bullish
Description:
A resistance breakout occurs when a stock's price closes decisively above a previously established resistance level — the price ceiling where sellers historically stepped in. This is one of the most powerful signals in technical analysis.

How to identify:
- Price has tested the same high (resistance) at least twice before.
- The breakout candle closes above that level, preferably with volume 20%+ above average.
- Volume confirmation is critical; a breakout on low volume is often a "false breakout."

What it means:
- The supply zone has been absorbed. Sellers who previously capped the price have been overwhelmed by buyers.
- Former resistance now becomes new support.
- The stock may continue upward by roughly the same distance as the base/consolidation pattern (measured move).

Historical significance:
- Studies show volume-confirmed breakouts have a higher success rate (60–70%+) in trending markets.
- In Indian equities, breakouts often coincide with earnings surprises, FII inflows, or sector tailwinds.

Key trading levels:
- Entry: On the breakout candle close or on a pullback to the breakout level.
- Stop-loss: Below the former resistance (now support).
- Target: Resistance + height of the base.

---

PATTERN: SUPPORT BOUNCE
Type: Support/Resistance
Direction: Bullish
Description:
A support bounce occurs when a stock's price falls to a key support level and reverses upward. Support levels are price floors where buyers have historically stepped in.

How to identify:
- Price approaches or touches a well-established support zone.
- A reversal candle forms at or near the support (hammer, bullish engulfing, doji).
- Ideally accompanied by declining sell-side volume and rising buy-side volume.

What it means:
- Buyers are defending a critical price level; demand exceeds supply at this zone.
- Risk/reward is favourable — stop-loss is tight (just below support), while target is the prior high.

Key trading levels:
- Entry: On the reversal candle confirmation.
- Stop-loss: A daily close below support.
- Target: Previous swing high or next resistance zone.

PATTERN: DOUBLE TOP
Type: Reversal
Direction: Bearish
Description:
The double top is a bearish reversal pattern formed after an uptrend. It consists of two peaks at approximately the same price level, separated by a trough (the neckline).

How to identify:
- Two highs formed within 3% of each other.
- A trough (neckline) between the two peaks.
- Price breaks below the neckline to confirm the pattern.
- Volume is typically lower on the second peak, signalling weakening momentum.

What it means:
- The stock has twice failed to break above a price ceiling — buyers are exhausted.
- The breakdown below the neckline signals a shift from uptrend to downtrend.
- Measured move target: Neckline − (Peak − Neckline).

Key trading levels:
- Entry: On close below the neckline.
- Stop-loss: Above the second peak.
- Target: Neckline minus the height of the pattern.

Common mistakes:
- Entering before the neckline breaks (anticipated pattern, not confirmed).
- Ignoring volume; a neckline break on low volume is suspicious.

---

PATTERN: DOUBLE BOTTOM
Type: Reversal
Direction: Bullish
Description:
The double bottom is the bullish mirror of the double top. It forms after a downtrend and signals a potential reversal to the upside.

How to identify:
- Two troughs at approximately the same price level (within 3%).
- A peak (neckline) between the two troughs.
- Price breaks above the neckline on volume to confirm.
- Volume is often higher on the second bottom's recovery, showing growing buyer conviction.

What it means:
- The stock has twice found buyers at a key level — sellers are losing control.
- Neckline breakout triggers institutional buying as the pattern becomes confirmed.
- Measured move target: Neckline + (Neckline − Trough).

Key trading levels:
- Entry: On close above the neckline.
- Stop-loss: Below the second trough.
- Target: Neckline plus the height of the pattern.

PATTERN: BULL FLAG
Type: Continuation
Direction: Bullish
Description:
The bull flag is a short-term continuation pattern that forms after a sharp, impulsive price surge (the "pole"), followed by a brief, orderly consolidation (the "flag") before the uptrend resumes.

How to identify:
- A strong rally of 6%+ over a short period (the pole).
- A tight consolidation with slightly downward or horizontal drift (the flag) — typically 3–5 sessions.
- Flag range should be less than 6% of the pole high.
- Breakout above the upper flag boundary confirms continuation.

What it means:
- The initial surge attracted buyers. The consolidation is healthy profit-taking.
- The tight, orderly pullback shows sellers are not aggressive — buyers will likely step in again.
- The expected move after breakout equals roughly the height of the pole.

Key trading levels:
- Entry: On breakout above the flag's upper trendline.
- Stop-loss: Below the flag's lower trendline.
- Target: Flag breakout point + pole height.

PATTERN: BEARISH RSI DIVERGENCE
Type: Divergence
Direction: Bearish
Description:
Bearish RSI divergence occurs when price makes higher highs, but the RSI (Relative Strength Index) makes lower highs simultaneously. It is a warning signal that the uptrend is losing momentum and may reverse.

How to identify:
- Price chart: Higher high.
- RSI chart (14-period): Lower high.
- RSI should be in or above the overbought zone (typically above 60–70).

What it means:
- Price is being pushed up, but momentum (as measured by RSI) is weakening.
- Fewer buyers are participating in the rally — the move is losing steam.
- Often precedes a pullback or trend reversal, particularly if the RSI later breaks below 50.

Important caveats:
- Divergence alone is not a sell signal; wait for price confirmation (break of a trendline or key support).
- In strong uptrends, divergence can persist for many bars before resolution.

---

PATTERN: BULLISH RSI DIVERGENCE
Type: Divergence
Direction: Bullish
Description:
Bullish RSI divergence occurs when price makes lower lows, but RSI makes higher lows simultaneously. It signals that selling pressure is exhausting and a reversal to the upside may be near.

How to identify:
- Price chart: Lower low.
- RSI chart (14-period): Higher low.
- RSI typically in or approaching oversold territory (below 40–45).

What it means:
- Price is still falling, but the rate of decline is slowing — sellers are losing conviction.
- Smart money / institutional buyers are quietly accumulating.
- A confirming bullish candle or breakout above a key level is the trigger to act.

HEAD AND SHOULDERS
Type: Reversal
Direction: Bearish
Description:
Head and Shoulders is one of the most reliable and widely recognised reversal patterns. It forms at market tops after a prolonged uptrend.

Structure:
- Left Shoulder: A peak, followed by a pullback.
- Head: A higher peak (the highest point), followed by a deeper pullback to the neckline.
- Right Shoulder: A lower peak, roughly equal to the left shoulder.
- Neckline: A horizontal or slightly sloping line connecting the two troughs.

What it means:
- The pattern shows three pushes higher, each weaker than the last — classic buyer exhaustion.
- The neckline break is the trigger; the price target is measured as: Neckline − (Head − Neckline).

---

INVERSE HEAD AND SHOULDERS
Type: Reversal
Direction: Bullish
Description:
The inverse head and shoulders forms at market bottoms after a downtrend and signals a bullish reversal.

Structure:
- Left Shoulder: A trough, followed by a recovery.
- Head: A deeper trough (the lowest point), followed by a recovery to neckline.
- Right Shoulder: A shallower trough, roughly equal to the left shoulder.
- Neckline: Connects the two peaks between the shoulders.

What it means:
- Three pushes lower, each with less selling power — sellers running out of steam.
- Neckline break confirms the reversal. Target: Neckline + (Neckline − Head).

---

CUP AND HANDLE
Type: Continuation
Direction: Bullish
Description:
The cup and handle is a bullish continuation pattern resembling the profile of a tea cup. It was popularised by William O'Neil.

Structure:
- Cup: A U-shaped rounding bottom (gradual decline and recovery, not V-shaped).
- Handle: A brief, mild pullback (5–15%) just below the prior high.
- Breakout: Price breaks above the handle resistance with volume.

What it means:
- The cup represents a period of constructive base building.
- The handle shakes out weak holders before the next leg up.
- Often associated with strong fundamental stocks making new highs.
